10 SEO Agency Red Flags
Every US Business Should Know.

After 15 years inside the US SEO industry — at agencies, in-house, and consulting — here are the warning signs that tell you an agency is not what it claims to be.

We have been inside the US agency industry for 15 years. We have seen the playbook. These red flags are not hypothetical — they are things we watched happen to real clients.

Red flag 1: They guarantee specific rankings

No legitimate SEO agency guarantees first-page rankings. Google's algorithm has hundreds of signals. No one controls it. Agencies that guarantee 'page one in 30 days' are either lying about what they can deliver, or planning to use tactics that will eventually get your site penalized.

Red flag 2: They cannot explain what they are doing in plain English

If an agency uses jargon to explain deliverables and cannot tell you, in simple terms, what they are building and why it will generate leads — that jargon is a smoke screen. Real expertise enables clear explanation, not obscures it.

Red flag 3: They report on metrics that do not connect to revenue

'Domain Authority increased by 5 points.' 'We published 8 blog posts this month.' 'Impressions are up 40%.' These are activity metrics. The only metrics that matter are rankings for buyer-intent keywords, organic traffic, and leads or revenue from organic. If your monthly report is full of the former and silent on the latter, you are being managed, not served.

Red flag 4: They outsource your work without telling you

This is the most common and most damaging practice in the US agency industry. An agency charges $4,000/mo, outsources execution to a $10/hr team, and pockets the margin. You have no idea the work is not being done by the team you met during the sales process. Ask directly: who is doing the work? Get it in writing.

Red flag 5: Their contracts are longer than their case studies

12-month minimum contracts are a signal that an agency does not believe their results speak for themselves. Legitimate results create client retention without contractual obligation. If an agency needs a year-long contract to keep your business, ask yourself why.

Red flag 6: They do not mention AI or GEO

In 2026, any SEO agency that does not have a clear answer to 'how do you optimize for ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini?' is behind. AI search is 40% of information discovery and growing. An agency ignoring GEO is ignoring 40% of the channel they are supposed to be managing.

Red flag 7: The 'phase 2' that never seems to arrive

'Phase 1 is foundation work — you will see results in phase 2.' This is a common delay tactic. Legitimate SEO shows measurable progress within 60-90 days. If you are six months in and still being told results are in the next phase, you are being strung along.

Red flag 8: They upsell on every call

Every quarterly call surfaces a new 'opportunity' that requires additional budget. Sometimes it is legitimate. When it happens every single quarter without corresponding results, it is a revenue extraction strategy, not a growth strategy.

Red flag 9: They cannot show you the actual work

You should be able to see every piece of content they produce, every link they build, every technical fix they implement — before and after. If an agency is vague about deliverables or protective about 'proprietary methods,' ask for proof of work. If they cannot produce it, they are not producing it.

Red flag 10: No clear answer on who owns your content and data

Some agencies retain ownership of content, link profiles, or analytics setups they build for you — meaning if you leave, you lose everything. Every asset built for your brand should be owned by your brand from day one. Get this in writing before you sign anything.

How do I evaluate an SEO agency before hiring?
Ask for three specific case studies with verifiable results. Ask who will do the actual work. Ask what metrics they report on and how those connect to revenue. Ask what happens to your assets if you cancel. Ask how they approach GEO. The quality of their answers tells you everything.
What should a fair SEO contract look like?
Month-to-month with 30 days notice to cancel. Clear deliverables listed for each month. Explicit statement that all content, link profiles, and data belong to the client. No performance guarantees, but clear KPIs. No mandatory minimum spend increases.
Is there any reason a 12-month contract is legitimate?
Some link-building programs require longer timelines to show full results. But even then, month-to-month with a transparent roadmap is better than a locked contract. If an agency insists on 12 months with no exit clause, walk away.
